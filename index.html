Framework

Three lenses for reading a crisis

Each lens explains a different part of how a political emergency unfolds online.

Agenda-setting

What the public thinks about

Media attention signals which issues matter. On social platforms, the agenda is set jointly by journalists, officials and ordinary users.

McCombs and Shaw, 1972
Framing

How the issue is understood

Frames select and stress certain aspects of events. Competing frames, such as defence of order or attack on democracy, compete for public acceptance.

Entman, 1993
Information disorder

What happens when facts are contested

Misinformation, disinformation and malinformation differ in intent and harm. All three spread fastest when official explanations lag.

Wardle and Derakhshan, 2017

Principles

Five pillars of effective crisis communication

Institutions that hold all five are better placed to keep public trust.

Speed

Fill the silence before rumour does.

Accuracy

Verified facts with clear sources.

Transparency

Explain the reasoning behind decisions.

Consistency

One account across every channel.

Empathy

Acknowledge public fear and loss.
